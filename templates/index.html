Machine Learning Architecture

Understanding the Prediction Engine

How gradient boosted decision trees and polynomial feature engineering capture complex medical risk drivers.

What Is Insurance Cost Prediction?

Health insurance underwriting involves forecasting future healthcare claims for an individual based on known risk parameters. Machine learning models identify non-linear relationships across thousands of patient profiles to forecast realistic expected annual healthcare costs without arbitrary human bias.

XGBoost & Random Forest

XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting): An ensemble algorithm that builds trees sequentially. Each new tree corrects the residual errors of prior trees with regularization, yielding top-tier predictive accuracy.

Random Forest: An ensemble of parallel bagging decision trees that average predictions to control variance and prevent overfitting.

The Role of Feature Engineering

Real-world medical risks compound non-linearly:

  • smoker_bmi: An interaction term. Smoking at a normal BMI increases cost, but smoking with high BMI creates exponential medical risk.
  • age_bmi: Reflects that high BMI becomes significantly more dangerous with advancing biological age.
  • age² & bmi²: Capture polynomial quadratic risk curves where risk accelerates rather than increases in a straight line.

Model Evaluation Metrics Explained

Rigorous regression metrics quantify model reliability:

  • R² Score (Coefficient of Determination): The proportion of variance in medical charges explained by our model (closer to 1.0 is superior).
  • MAE (Mean Absolute Error): The average dollar amount by which predictions deviate from actual historical charges.
  • RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error): Penalizes larger individual errors more heavily than MAE.
